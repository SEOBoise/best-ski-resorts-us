The Best Ski Resorts in the US: Explore the Top Winter Destinations

The best ski resorts in the United States depend on the trip you want to take. Compare terrain for your ability level, lodging access, lift tickets or pass eligibility, and the journey from the airport to the mountain. Start with the resort comparison below, then use our destination guides and each resort’s current snow report to check the details for your dates.

This guide focuses on U.S. destinations rather than trying to cover Canada, Europe or other international ski regions. It is a research-based comparison for skiers, snowboarders and families deciding among different mountain experiences. Conditions, lift operations, parking rules, pass access and lodging availability change, so treat every trip-specific detail as something to recheck before booking.

How We Choose and Compare Resorts

We compare resorts by terrain variety, lift network, suitability for different ability levels, destination access, lodging logistics and the quality of information a traveler can verify. A featured resort needs a clear reason to consider it and a meaningful tradeoff. We do not assign an overall winner, convert promotional language into a score or assume that the largest mountain is automatically the right mountain.

Official mountain information is the first source for terrain definitions, lift counts, trail maps and operating conditions. We separate stable mountain facts from changing details such as ticket prices, pass eligibility, parking requirements, open terrain and snowfall on a particular date. The comparison reflects desk research rather than an assertion that the editor personally tested every resort.

Featured Ski Resorts

Sugarloaf, Maine

Sugarloaf publishes 1,360 developed, skiable acres, a 2,820-foot vertical drop, 176 trails and glades, and 15 lifts. Those definitions matter: the figure describes developed skiable terrain rather than a larger permitted boundary. West Mountain adds a distinct beginner and intermediate zone, while the main mountain, Snowfields, Brackett Basin and Burnt Mountain create very different route choices for experienced groups.

The practical tradeoff is access. Sugarloaf is a destination drive, so compare flight options, winter road time, parking and slopeside versus off-mountain lodging before choosing dates. Lift tickets, dining and accommodation should be evaluated separately; a day ticket is not presented here as a meal benefit. Check the current trail map and mountain report before travel.

Steamboat Resort, Colorado

Steamboat Resort in northwest Colorado spans mountain areas including Mount Werner, Sunshine Peak, Storm Peak, Thunderhead Peak, Mahogany Ridge and Christie Peak. Current mountain statistics list 3,741 permitted acres, 184 trails, 23 lifts and a 3,668-foot vertical rise. The resort-specific Champagne Powder name belongs with Steamboat; it is not used here as a general description of snow at unrelated mountains.

Use the trail map to match terrain to your group instead of treating the resort as one uniform slope. Wild Blue Gondola reaches Sunshine Peak, and the lift network connects learning areas, groomers, bowls and glades across a large footprint. Review current lift operations, ticket or pass access, parking or transit and Yampa Valley transportation before choosing travel dates.

Cannon Mountain, New Hampshire

Cannon Mountain and Mittersill form a New Hampshire ski area with 287 skiable acres, 98 trails and glades, and 9 lifts. Its north and northeast exposure, long vertical and mix of intermediate and advanced terrain make it a terrain-led choice rather than a self-contained resort-village vacation.

That distinction helps set expectations. Travelers commonly pair the mountain with lodging elsewhere in the White Mountains and drive to the base. Check wind, visibility, open lifts, trail status and designated parking before leaving. Families and beginners should identify the learning terrain and operating lifts on the day’s report rather than relying on a broad “family resort” label.

Whitefish Mountain Resort, Montana

Whitefish Mountain Resort is a Montana destination with 3,000 acres, a 2,353-foot vertical drop, 110 named trails and a current lift network of 14 lifts. Bowls and glades support tree-skiing trips, while groomed trails, terrain parks and beginner conveyors serve different ability levels. All of those Montana details belong together in the Whitefish profile.

Whitefish is not in the Adirondacks and should not be confused with Whiteface Mountain. For access, compare Glacier Park International Airport, the Whitefish rail station, local transport and parking. Lodging in town and lodging near the mountain create different shuttle and dining needs. Use the current snow report to distinguish long-term snowfall averages from what is actually open.

Whiteface Mountain, New York

Whiteface Mountain is a separate New York destination in the Adirondacks near Lake Placid. It publishes a 3,430-foot vertical, 94 trails across three peaks and an 11-lift system. Long groomed routes, steeper terrain and the conditional expert terrain of The Slides create a different experience from Whitefish’s Montana bowls and glades.

Lake Placid provides a broader lodging and dining base, while Wilmington is closer to the mountain. Compare that tradeoff with the morning drive, parking and the day’s lift plan. The Slides and other natural terrain open only when conditions and patrol decisions allow, so a historical snow total cannot substitute for the current conditions report.

Brundage Mountain Resort, Idaho

Brundage Mountain Resort near McCall lists 1,920 lift-accessed acres, a 1,921-foot vertical drop, 70 named trails and 6 lifts. Its mix includes the Easy Street learning area, broad groomers, glades and more demanding natural terrain. The resort states that 420 acres within the lift-accessed total are unpatrolled and do not receive avalanche mitigation, a material distinction for advanced travelers.

Before traveling, confirm snow and grooming reports, lift status, ticket rules, the McCall shuttle schedule and winter parking. Travelers flying into Boise should also account for the mountain drive and changing road conditions. The official mountain link appears in the comparison section above.

Find the Right Resort for Your Trip

Beginners and families

Start with the learning-area layout, lesson meeting point, beginner lifts and the route back to lodging or parking. Sugarloaf’s West Mountain and Brundage’s Easy Street area are examples of zones that deserve closer review, but no resort is automatically best for every family. Compare child-care availability, lesson age rules, room configuration, meal options and how much walking or shuttle travel the day requires.

Terrain-focused trips

Advanced skiers should compare the kind of difficulty, not merely the percentage of black terrain. Cannon’s steeper state-park experience, Whiteface’s conditional Slides, Whitefish’s bowls and glades, and Steamboat’s large tree-skiing network create different demands. Use the current trail map, lift status and patrol guidance; never interpret an acreage number as permission to enter closed or uncontrolled terrain.

Value and access

Total trip value includes the lift product, lodging, ground transport, parking, meals, rentals and route time. A lower ticket price can be offset by a longer transfer or a rental car, while a higher room rate may include walkable lift access. Compare the same dates and traveler count, then record what is included rather than relying on an advertised starting price.

Keep destination identities precise while comparing value. Aspen Snowmass is a Colorado destination built around Aspen Mountain, Aspen Highlands, Buttermilk and Snowmass, while Lake Tahoe is a region containing separate resorts such as Palisades Tahoe, Heavenly and Kirkwood. Deer Valley and Snowbasin are distinct Utah resorts with their own terrain, lodging, access and operating policies. The regional directory below preserves those individual destination pages.

Plan Tickets, Transport and Lodging

Lift tickets and passes

Compare date-specific lift tickets with the exact Ikon Pass or Epic Pass product you may use. Resort access, restricted dates, available days and reservation rules can differ by season and product. Other resorts use independent or regional passes. Confirm the named resort, winter season and traveler eligibility instead of assuming every pass in a brand family provides the same access.

Airports, transfers and parking

An airport code does not describe the entire winter journey. Compare scheduled flights with ground-transfer availability, rental-car requirements, mountain-road exposure and realistic arrival time. Glacier Park International Airport is relevant to Whitefish, Yampa Valley Regional Airport to Steamboat, and Boise is a common starting point for Brundage travelers. Sugarloaf, Cannon and Whiteface involve different regional drives. Parking can be free, paid, limited or reservation-based depending on resort, lot and date, so check the current policy and shuttle alternative.

Hotels, condos and other accommodation

Location changes the trip. Slopeside hotels or condos can reduce morning transport, while town lodging may offer more dining and room configurations. A chalet may be useful for a larger group only when it is a real local accommodation option and the transport plan works. Record whether the property is walkable, ski-in/ski-out, on a shuttle route or dependent on a car, and verify cancellation terms before booking.

Snow Conditions and Travel Timing

Historical snowfall describes climate, not the snow a traveler will encounter. A current snow report should separate new snow, base depth, surface description, open terrain and lift status. Wind, visibility, avalanche work, snowmaking and maintenance can change operations after a report is posted. Use the timestamp and check again on the morning of travel.

Season dates are also conditional. Opening and closing plans depend on weather, coverage, staffing and the terrain a resort can operate safely. Weekends and holidays can increase crowds, but no guide can promise short lift lines on a specific date. Travelers who prioritize quieter periods should compare midweek dates while also checking which lifts, lessons, lodging and transit services operate then.
